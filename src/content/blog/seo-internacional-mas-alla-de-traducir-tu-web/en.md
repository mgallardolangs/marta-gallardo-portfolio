I'm a translator and I'm currently taking an International SEO course. I've done three lessons and have ten to go, but it has already left me with a clear conclusion: before translating a website into several languages, you need to decide which markets make sense for you. Here is what I've understood so far, in case you run a business that wants to sell abroad and you're not sure where to start.

## What Is International SEO, and Why Isn't Translation Enough?

International SEO means preparing your website so that search engines show each version to the right audience, depending on their country and language. Translating your content into as many languages as possible is the most visible part, but on its own it isn't a strategy. The hard part is building an expansion plan that makes sense. That strategy begins before the translation, by studying each country and language where your website could fit.

## Country and Language: The Two Variables Everything Revolves Around

The first thing that became clear to me is that there are two variables, and they are not the same thing: country and language. As a translator, I see this clearly with Spanish, which is spoken in many countries, each with different customs, vocabulary and ways of searching. Choosing a language is not the same as choosing a market.

That is why the course suggests keeping a register of countries: which ones it would make sense for you to enter, in which ones you could make a profit, and what logistical and legal advantages or difficulties each one has. Language is just one more item on that list.

## Three Questions to Assess a Market

The course asks three questions that I found very useful for deciding whether a country deserves its own version of your website:

1. Do your audience's search terms and search behaviour change significantly from one country to another?
2. Does your product, service or information change depending on the country? In other words, does location influence your business?
3. Is there enough search traffic and conversion potential for that country's version to deliver a positive return on investment?

The next step is to add all the potential markets to an assessment sheet and choose which ones to analyse in depth. Keyword research is carried out only for those, to understand how much opportunity and how much competition there is.

## Fewer Markets, Chosen Better

Another idea I'm taking away is that international SEO needs a budget. You need someone to carry out research, translate the content you already have, publish new content on an ongoing basis and look after customers in each market. Even if you lean heavily on AI, it can quickly become expensive.

Since resources are usually limited, the recommendation is to focus on fewer markets, but choose them better. I think this is a realistic approach for any business that is starting to expand: begin with a few countries that make sense and grow from there once they are working.
