Before reviewing a client's website, I wanted to go through the same process myself. That's why, on 14 November, I ran a full SEO audit on marttelier.com. In this post I'll tell you what was wrong, what I changed and what the data shows ten days later (including what still isn't working). If your website is in several languages, you'll probably recognise some of these problems.


## The tools I used

You don't need to pay to get started. I used three free tools:

- **Screaming Frog** (free version, up to 500 URLs). It crawls your site the way Google does and shows you titles, H1s, redirects and broken links.
- **Ahrefs Webmaster Tools.** It runs a Site Audit and gives you your Domain Rating, which measures how much authority your domain has based on the links it gets.
- **Google Search Console.** It tells you which pages Google has indexed and why it leaves others out.

## What was wrong

**1. Six languages, three of them neglected.** My site had Spanish, English, French, German, Italian and Catalan versions. The German, Italian and Catalan ones were unreviewed translations: 36 pages that no other page on the site linked to, yet they were in the sitemap and the hreflang tags. As a translator, that's exactly what I advise against: publishing a language you can't maintain.

**2. Repeated H1s.** Some pages had their main heading three times, so Google couldn't tell what they were about.

**3. Titles that didn't say what I do.** The homepage title was "Marta Gallardo | Consultora SEO Freelance". It didn't say where I work or that I also do SEO translation. On top of that, ten titles were over 60 characters and Google cut them off in the results.

**4. No visitor data.** I didn't have Google Analytics installed, so I didn't know how many people visited the site.

**5. A scattered message.** The homepage sold SEO, translation and UGC all at once, with vague lines like "Comprehensive solutions to scale your brand". Neither Google nor a potential client could tell what I was the best choice for.

## What I changed

- **I unpublished three languages.** I kept Spanish, English and French, the ones I can write and review myself. The German and Italian pages redirect with a 301 to the English version, and the Catalan ones to Spanish. That way nothing Google already knew gets lost.
- **I cleaned up the sitemap and hreflang.** The sitemap now only lists URLs in the three languages, and each page points to itself and to its two translations.
- **I changed the blog URLs.** Each post has a URL in its own language (for example, /en/blog/seo-translation instead of the Spanish slug). The old ones redirect to the new ones.
- **I fixed the H1s and rewrote the titles.** The homepage is now titled "Consultora SEO y traducción SEO en Elche | Marttelier". Every title is 60 characters or fewer, and the legal pages have their own descriptions.
- **I installed Google Analytics** with a cookie banner.
- **I redesigned the home, About and service pages** to focus the message on SEO and SEO translation.
- **I completed my Google Business Profile**, which was already verified.

## The data: before and after

| | 14 November | 27 November |
|---|---|---|
| Published languages | 6 | 3 |
| URLs in the sitemap | Mixed 6 languages | Spanish, English and French only |
| Pages with no internal links | 36 | 0 |
| Titles over 60 characters | 10 | 0 |
| Google Analytics | No | Yes |
| Pages indexed by Google | Not checked | 86 |
| Domain Rating (Ahrefs) | 0 | 0 |

Search Console shows 36 pages not indexed, but 27 of them show up as "Page with redirect". That's what should happen: they're the old URLs I redirected, and Google will gradually drop them.

## What still isn't working

The technical side is clean, but my site still doesn't rank. And it's not a technical problem:

- **It lacks authority.** The Domain Rating is still 0 because hardly any websites link to mine. In fact, if you search "marttelier" on Google, it doesn't show up yet.
- **It has little content.** With four posts, Google doesn't see me as a source on any topic yet.

Fixing the technical side took me ten days. Authority and content take months: new posts every month and links from other websites. I'll measure everything again in mid-December and share the results.

## How to check your website in four steps

If your website is in several languages, start here:

1. Search `site:yourwebsite.com` on Google and look at which pages and titles appear.
2. Open Search Console → Pages and read why some pages aren't indexed. "Page with redirect" isn't a problem. On the other hand, "Crawled – currently not indexed" is worth a closer look.
3. Ask yourself whether you can really maintain every language you've published. A neglected language does more harm than good.
4. Check that your titles say what you do and where, in under 60 characters.
